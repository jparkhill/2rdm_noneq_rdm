# Where the work stands, 2026-08-09

## What was established

**Term census of the rank-k EOM**, exact at ranks 1 through 4: `2k` one-body and `2k(k-1)`
two-body terms carry `gamma_k`; `4k` two-body terms carry `gamma_{k+1}`; never any one-body at
`k+1`. Rank 3 was registered in `wick.py` and `gwt.py` and validated against `qdiss.eom.fullci` at
`8.7e-16` before use.

**The drift is free.** The `2k^2` terms carrying `gamma_k` form a Lyapunov map `A X + X A^dag`
(rank-1 probe at ranks 2 and 3: output rank exactly 2, residuals `~2e-16`), which compresses to
exactly zero on the kernel of the k-tuple matrix. So **all positivity risk lives in the `4k` terms
carrying `gamma_{k+1}`**, which is precisely what a reconstruction closure supplies. This is the
first genuinely order-general structural result of the programme.

**The exact closed-system condition is linear, not conic**: `P_K (d/dt Mat_k) P_K = 0`, with
`Mat_k(T)[I,J] = k! T[I+J]` over sorted k-tuples and `P_K` the projector onto its kernel. Verified
to machine precision at ranks 2 and 3.

**Why tangency and not inwardness: reversibility.** `t -> v^dag X(t) v` is non-negative and
vanishes at `t0`; two-sided existence makes that an interior minimum, forcing `v^dag Xdot v = 0`.
Unitary dynamics is invertible, so inward flow is *impossible*, not merely absent. This corrected
an earlier attribution to rank conservation, which was wrong.

**Imposing the condition works and is compatible with the trace identity.** Projecting `separable`
onto it at `(M,N) = (6,3)` took the residual `1.6e-01 -> 1.2e-15`, kept the rank-3 contraction
identity at `1.2e-16`, cost `3.8%` of the cumulant magnitude, and slightly improved accuracy.

## What was then deprecated, and why

**The whole tangency apparatus is a pure-state artifact.** Damping the Fock-basis coherences by
one part in `10^4` destroys the entire 9-dimensional kernel and the condition becomes *vacuous*;
clearance then grows linearly in the dephasing with slope `~0.108`. The mechanism is the rank
bound `rank Mat_k <= C(M,N-k)`, which holds only for pure states -- for mixed `rho` it becomes
`C(M,N-k) * rank(rho)` and is immediately exceeded. So the PSD boundary is touched only on a
measure-zero set and left instantly under any decoherence.

Consequently the kernel projection, and the Lipschitz study of its threshold (fine on each rank
stratum at `8.9e-02`, discontinuous across them at `2.2e+06`), describe a closed-system special
case. What survives from that work is the term census, the Lyapunov structure of the drift, and
the localisation of risk to the `4k` terms -- none of which need a kernel.

## The live question

**Vacuous is not safe.** At `s = 1e-4` dephasing the clearance is `1.09e-05` while closure
compressions are `O(0.5)`, which exhausts that margin in `~2e-05` time units. The bath converts
the problem from a boundary condition into a **rate competition**: the bath restores clearance at
a rate set by its strength, the closure erodes it at a rate set by its defect, and positivity
survives when the former exceeds the latter.

So `gamma*` -- the weakest bath keeping a given closure positive -- remains the right figure of
merit, redefined as a rate balance in the interior rather than an eigenvalue condition on a face.
It never identifies a kernel, so it has no threshold and sidesteps the Lipschitz problem. It has
units of a rate, so the criterion is `gamma* < gamma_phys`, the molecule's actual relaxation rate:
the bath stops being a tunable knob and becomes a measurement of the system.

**First measurement, from `tools/bath_spectra.py`:** TDHF on H3/sto-3g has mean pole width
`-0.0251` at zero bath, i.e. an intrinsically growing mode, turning `+0.0249` at `g = 0.05` and
`+0.5251` at `g = 0.5`. So `gamma* ~ 0.05` atomic units for TDHF, and the pole error stayed at
`0.0139` across the sweep -- the bath stabilised the flow without moving the frequencies.

## Immediate next experiment, with its three known defects to fix

Poles and positivity versus bath strength from the FCI density. The harness exists in
`tools/bath_spectra.py`; its first run was not trustworthy for three reasons, all documented in
its docstring:

1. **Kick the wavefunction with a dipole operator**, `exp(-i eps mu)|Psi>`, not `gamma_2`
   directly. The direct kick is not N-representable and dominated every positivity and trace
   metric.
2. **Use a system without a degenerate FCI spectrum** -- the asymmetric trapezoid H4, with the
   Jacobian on `qdiss.gpu`, since the `M=8` numpy Jacobian did not finish. H3 gave only two
   distinct frequencies among five targets.
3. **Report positivity against the exact clearance** at the same state, not against zero.

## Open, in priority order

1. The corrected bath/spectra experiment above, giving `gamma*` per closure.
2. Construct the `sum_i L_i gamma_k L_i^dag` parameterisation of the `4k` terms, giving a closure
   tangent by FORM rather than by projection -- hence Lipschitz and provably safe at all states.
3. Does a bath make the hierarchy CONVERGE, i.e. does `|lambda_{k+1}|/|lambda_k|` fall with
   coupling? Magnitude alone does not justify truncation. Never measured.
4. Propagate a Cholesky factor `R` with `X = R^dag R`, so positivity is structural in the
   representation. Matters most in the closed case, where tangent flow gives no restoring force.
5. `Q` and `G` cones have their own conditions; `G` is the one exactly singular at the pure ground
   state, so it may be the binding one.
6. Propagate only the projection of each cumulant that the lower rank can see -- the visible
   fraction is `[k/(M-k+1)]^2`, and the question is whether the induced Krylov space closes.

## Closure status, for reference

`blocked` is retired: pole surface `a_i = (1/2) sum_j a_j` inside the feasible set,
non-extensivity from global sums, and no `V`-dependence at all. `pt` (the reference-free adiabatic
amplitude) reproduces MP1/MP2 exactly at an idempotent density but is no better than `lambda_2 = 0`
away from the pure ground state, and needs a level shift `sigma ~ 1e-2` to pass the Lipschitz
screen -- which costs nothing in accuracy. `trace_plus_pt` is the best on energy and cumulant error
but was only validated along a thermal path. `muller_rdmft` is poor throughout.
